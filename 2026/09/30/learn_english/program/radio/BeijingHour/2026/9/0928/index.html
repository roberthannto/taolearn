This year marks the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Long March of the Chinese Workers’ and Peasants’ Red Army. The Long March was an epic military trek from 1934 to 1936. Led by the Communist Party of China, the Red Army broke through a Kuomintang siege. The Long March route ran as far as 12,500 kilometers. In October 1934, the Central Red Army left Ruijin in eastern China’s Jiangxi province. More than 86,000 soldiers assembled at Yudu and crossed the Yudu River. The Long March had begun. After the battle at the Xiangjiang River, only 30,000 remained, but the journey did not end.

In January 1935, a crucial meeting in Zunyi in southwest China’s Guizhou marked a turning point for the army. Later, the Red Army crossed the Chishui River four times, traversed the Jinsha River, forced a crossing of the Dadu River, and captured the Luding Bridge. The army braved raging rivers, snow-covered mountains, and treacherous grasslands. In October 1935, the Central Red Army reached Shaanxi in northern China. Other Red Army forces were also on the move. In October 1936, the Second and Fourth Front Armies reached Huining in Gansu province and joined forces with the First Front Army. The rendezvous of the three front armies marked the end of the Long March. Our reporter has traveled to Yudu county in southeastern Jiangxi province, the starting point of the Long March, to retrace the footsteps of Red Army soldiers and to see how this old revolutionary heartland has changed.

“A few strands of straw, twist it, weave, bound together, and little by little they become a pair of sandals. Now we’re here.”

“These are the straw sandals the Red Army wore. At that time, many of them didn’t even have shoes. They had to walk barefoot. It was really tough.”

For generations, these simple straw sandals were made by hand in the countryside of southern China. But here in Yudu, they carry another memory. 200,000 pairs of straw sandals were made here in Yudu as the Red Army gathered here to embark on the Long March. These simple sandals became one of the most practical gifts that people could offer. Yudu was one of the important gathering and departure points of the Central Red Army. In 1934, tens of thousands of people from Yudu joined the Red Army.

The story of the Long March has never been only about a past. For the people living here today, remembering the past also means building a future. Chinese President Xi Jinping visited Tantou Village during his 2019 trip to see how the village had charted a new path for development.

“The general secretary asked about my son’s job, like how much he earns, as well as the education of my grandkids. I told him that children now have good access to education, which is free of charge. They have lunch at school.”

For Sun Guanfa’s generation, the route looks very different from the one their ancestors traveled. No battlefields or endless mountain trails. Instead, there are villages to build and fields to cultivate. Chinese President Xi Jinping has said each generation has its own Long March. Here in Tantou Village, the Long March continues, not on the battlefields, but in the fields, where selenium-rich resources are helping fuel rural revitalization and bring new prosperity. The new journey is being written in the fields. Ganzhou officially began promoting the development of selenium-rich agricultural products after geological surveys in 2018. The village collective economic income grew from less than 80,000 yuan in 2018 to more than 3.55 million yuan in 2025. The road has changed, but the sandals still have stories to tell. Straw sandals may no longer be part of everyday life, but the spirit they carry lives on. In a different time, on a different path.

The tour guide is still very important with this information and storytelling abilities. Having a tour guide will help visitors ask questions and open discussions.”

What if you can’t visit Egypt? You would still enjoy its ancient treasures from afar, thanks to several apps that take you on a 3D tour from the comfort of your couch.

“During the Corona pandemic, we launched virtual museum apps to many museums, such as the Coptic Museum and the Islamic Museum. Virtual tours to ancient tombs in Luxor were launched as well. The success has allowed us to expand it further.”

Digital transformation is inevitable for the success of the tourism sector. Going forward, officials are planning to expand digital services for visitors at archaeological sites and ancient temples around the country, not just in museums.

Turning to sports. Asian record holder Yan Ziyi needed just one throw to win women’s javelin gold at the 2026 Asian Games, launching 70.46 meters to break the games record. The 18-year-old remains unbeaten across the five Diamond League meets this season. Japanese Paralympic champion Haruka Kitaguchi took silver.

In women’s long jump, China’s Huang Lingying came from behind with a sixth-round 6.55 meters to edge Ancy Sojan of India by 2 centimeters. Chen Yuanjiang won men’s 110-meter hurdles in 13.15 seconds, while Wu Yani took bronze in the women’s 100-meter hurdles. Peerapong Boonsong of Thailand equaled the Asian 200-meter record in 19.88 seconds.

China has claimed the artistic swimming team title at the 17th Asian Games, topping all three routines and extending its winning streak in the event to six consecutive editions. The team led a technical routine with a “Black Myth Wukong” performance, then premiered a new free routine themed “Time”. Their combined total of over 857 points secured gold, with Japan taking silver and Kazakhstan bronze. China also won the duet event, continuing its dominance in Asian artistic swimming.

In table tennis, Wang Manyu beat Chen Meng 4-2 in an all-Chinese final to win the women’s singles gold at the 2026 Asian Games. It’s Wang’s second Asian Games title, eight years after Jakarta 2018. In the men’s doubles final, Huang Yuzhen and Lin Shidong defeated a Japanese pair 4-2. Lin says they stayed focused and fought for every game. It’s Lin’s second gold after mixed doubles.

Chinese divers Lin Shan and Long Yiping have claimed the gold and silver in the women’s 1-meter springboard. Lin topped the podium with 263.05 points. China also swept both artistic swimming golds, with Lin Yanjun and Xu Huiyan winning the duet title.

We’ll learn lessons from the game and work hard to close the gap. If we want to make further progress, we have to defeat teams like New Zealand.”

At Milan Fashion Week, it’s not just Chinese designers who are showcasing their work. China’s textile industry is also taking center stage. A dedicated hub at the event is connecting fabric companies with global buyers and distributors.

“From the factory floor to the catwalk, China’s textile powerhouse Keqiao is increasingly making its mark at Milan Fashion Week. For the third time, Keqiao presented its collective runway show with emerging and established brands combining traditional and contemporary design. But its showcase goes beyond the runway. “Starting this collaboration with Keqiao, for us it is a key point. Because we can work with them to bring and to introduce the designer with the textile, with the fabrics, from China to the European and international market.”

For Italian designers, that means access to a range of materials and rapid production.

“Keqiao is a very important production hub in China. It’s very manageable and close to the designers. So for us, it is very easy and fast to test and choose materials.”

For the Yinan Group, textiles have been a family business for generations. Sally Tien is in Milan with her mother, and says they want to increasingly focus on design.

“It’s a kind of family business, but we have 2,000 employees to produce the fabric for Uniqlo and Zara and Disney. Yeah, but we also want to do some specific design, only designed by myself.”

“It’s a very special experience for us, and we already have our idea of how to improve for our brand during these days.”

“It’s a very important city for the pipeline from fiber to finished good.”

“We wanted to create a bridge between Italy and China. This is the first edition, and this is just the beginning.”
